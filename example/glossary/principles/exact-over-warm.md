# Priority

Hessling is a family company and sounds like one. When warmth makes a message less exact, the exact version wins.

# Example

A delivery delay for a roastery. Warm: "Good things take time, and your grinder is worth the wait." Exact: "Your grinder ships on 14 March, three weeks later than planned, because a burr batch failed inspection. Your price stays the same." The exact version goes out. A personal line from the account manager may follow it, never replace it.

# Where it stops

In consumer newsletters and gift messages, warmth may lead, as long as nothing in them is a claim or a promise.

# Why

Roasteries plan production around a delivery date. A warm message that hides a fact costs them money and costs Hessling trust that took decades to build.

# Rejected

- **Separate rules for consumer and trade.** Considered in 2025. Dropped because the collision happens inside single messages, for example a consumer asking about a delayed repair.
